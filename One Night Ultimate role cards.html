Mechanics to implementfor an online version

Everything an online game has to model, drawn from the official rulebooks. Details for each role, Mark and Artifact are in their panels further down.

Core loop and setup
  • A game is one night, one day discussion and one vote. There are no rounds, and the game ends when the vote is resolved.
  • Roles in play = players + 3. Any roles from any deck can be mixed. The three extra cards are dealt face down to the center (three fixed positions; Seer, Witch, Voodoo Lou, Exposer and others refer to them).
  • Player count: 3 to 10 with the base game. The #11 and #12 tokens in Bonus Roles raise it to 12 for Alien games.
  • Track two cards per player: the starting card (decides which call they act on) and the current card (decides role, team and win condition after the night). Both are hidden from other players until the reveal.
  • Some roles use an extra card outside the three center cards (the center Werewolf and the extra Villain card). Roles that view or move 'center cards' can also use the center Werewolf.
  • Player numbers: seats are numbered 1 to N in one direction around the table. Left/right neighbors, lower/higher numbers and odd/even all derive from this and are required by the Alien app rules.
Night phase and call order
  • Roles are called in one unified order using the call numbers. Negative numbers are Dusk and go first (for example Oracle -9, Copycat -8, Doppelganger -7, Vampires -6, Assassin -1), then 0 (Sentinel), 1 to 13. A letter suffix is called after the plain number in alphabetical order (2, 2-B, 2-C); SV and SVE follow F. Sort by number, then by suffix.
  • Only roles that are in the game are called. Roles without a call number are never called (they act only in the vote, or are passive).
  • Actions are bound to the starting card. If a card is moved, the player who started with the role still acts when it is called; the player who received the moved card is not told and does not act for it.
  • Roles that share a call act together and learn each other (Werewolves, Masons, Vampires, Aliens, Villains). Some groups reveal themselves to a later role, which should be modeled as an 'identify' event that gives the later role a list of players: Werewolves to the Minion and Squire, the Dream Wolf to the Werewolves, the Tanner to the Apprentice Tanner, the Seer and Apprentice Seer to the Beholder, Aliens to the Leader, the Mad Scientist to the Intern.
  • Action types: view (a player's card, a center card, a Mark), swap (cards or Marks, seen or unseen), move (shift all cards), reveal (turn a card face up for everyone), place (shield, Artifact, Mark), signal (Thing, Annoying Lad), and team choice (Nostradamus). Results are private to the actor unless the role says otherwise.
  • Unseen swaps: players whose cards or Marks are swapped by someone else are not told. After the night a player's role is the card now in front of them, which may differ from the card they started with.
  • Copy roles (Doppelganger, Copycat, Mirror Man) adopt the identity of the card they view and may be called again later; the exact calls are on each card.
  • Phase sequence: Dusk roles act, then every player views their own Mark (not their card), then the Lovers wake (call 00), then the regular night calls run from 0 upward.
Day phase, voting and deaths
  • Discussion has a timer. Players may say anything, including lies, may not reveal their cards, and may not look at any cards after the night. Artifacts and ripples can restrict discussion (Mask of Muting mutes a player, Shroud of Shame blocks their view, some ripples shorten the timer or silence odd or even players).
  • Voting: every player simultaneously votes for one other player. Count the voters per target.
  • Base resolution: the player with the most votes dies and their card is revealed. Ties kill all tied players. If no player has more than one vote, nobody dies.
  • Vote modifiers are hooks in the resolution pipeline: ignored votes, protection of a chosen player (the next most voted player with more than one vote dies instead), redirecting the catch, chained deaths (Hunter, Lovers), voter-triggered conversion (Cursed, Windy Wendy), win restrictions on voters (Mark of Disease), and instant wins (Leader). Each role's panel gives its exact rule.
  • Recommended resolution order for an implementation: tally votes, apply ignored votes and protections, determine deaths, apply chained deaths (Hunter, Lovers, repeated until stable), reveal cards, determine final teams, then check each team's win condition.
  • When a Bodyguard or Defender-er is in play, all cards must be revealed right after the vote so the protected player can be checked against the vote totals.
  • Hero-style decks use the word 'caught' instead of 'killed'; the logic is identical.
Teams, final roles and win checks
  • Final team: start from the current card. A Mark can change the team (Mark of the Vampire makes a Vampire). Artifacts that change the role override the card and any Mark, and other Marks then apply to the new role. A token matching your card changes nothing.
  • Teams: Village, Werewolf (including Minion, Squire, Alpha, Mystic and Dream Wolf), Vampire (including Renfield), Alien, Hero, Villain, own-team roles (Tanner, Apprentice Tanner, Assassin, Apprentice Assassin, Synthetic Alien, Blob, Mortician, Family Man, Mad Scientist and Intern), no team (Innocent Bystander, wins with the Heroes), and Nostradamus, whose team is the team of the last card he viewed.
  • Base win conditions: Village wins if at least one Werewolf dies, or if nobody is a Werewolf and nobody dies. Werewolves win only if at least one player is a Werewolf and no Werewolf dies. Special cases for the Minion and Tanner are on their panels.
  • Teams that win in addition to others: Assassin, Apprentice Assassin, Blob, Mortician and Family Man. Several teams can win in the same game.
  • Epic Battles: when three or more of Village, Werewolf, Vampire and Alien are active (held by at least one player, not only in the center), at least two players die: the most voted and the second most voted (one vote can count for second place). Ties for first kill only the tied players; a tie for second kills all of them. Each team then needs two conditions: Vampires need a Werewolf or Alien dead and no Vampire dead; Werewolves need a Vampire or Alien dead and no Werewolf dead; Aliens need a Werewolf or Vampire dead and no Alien dead; Village needs players from two different teams dead. If only two groups are active, only the player(s) with the most votes (at least two) die and normal conditions apply. If all Werewolves are in the center but a Minion or Squire is in play, only one player dies.
Marks
  • Every player starts with one Mark of Clarity. A player never has more than one Mark: when a Mark is given to a player, their old Mark is returned to the board unseen.
  • Marks can be placed (Vampire, Count, Renfield, Diseased, Cupid, Instigator, Priest, Assassin, Apprentice Assassin), viewed (Marksman) and swapped (Pickpocket, Gremlin). Each player views their own Mark once, after Dusk.
  • The Doppelganger uses a separate Doppelganger Mark set and there is no Doppelganger Mark of the Vampire.
Artifacts and shields
  • The Curator places a random hidden Artifact on any card, including his own, but not on a shielded card; a card holds at most one Artifact.
  • At the end of the night each holder privately learns their Artifact. They may say anything about it.
  • Role-changing Artifacts override the card and Mark and remove the old card's abilities. The others change how the holder takes part in the day or do nothing; see the Artifacts section.
  • Shield tokens (placed by the Sentinel) stop a card from being viewed, moved or given an Artifact; Marks are not protected.
App-driven variable rules (Alien)
  • The One Night app is mandatory for Alien: several roles cannot run without it, and each game it provides a different set of rules for those roles.
  • Variable roles receive their parameters from the app; the variation lists are on each card.
  • Ripples are random end-of-night effects that may happen when an Alien is in play, each with a chance of occurring and optionally forced by the Oracle.
  • If a player does not answer the app, or the role is in the center, the app chooses an answer. Requirements that cannot be met usually mean the action does nothing or falls back to a default question.
Hidden information and state
  • Per-player private knowledge: viewed cards and Marks, identified groups, Artifact contents, the Oracle's answer. Public events: revealed cards (Revealer, Exposer, Flipper), team announcements (Nostradamus), thumbs and signals only to the roles that should see them.
  • Role effects are applied to the final board state, not to the order of claims: for example Hunter, Bodyguard, Blob and Family Man effects use the card positions at the end of the night.

New mechanics

  • The core game: players + 3 cards, three center cards, one night, one day and a single vote.
  • Call order and acting by starting card, with secret views and unseen swaps.
  • Village, Werewolf and Tanner teams with the base win conditions.
  • Copying with the Doppelganger.
